Best Password Managers for Freelancers and Small Businesses

5 November 2026 · 1 min read

This article contains affiliate links. If you sign up through them we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. It never affects what we recommend.

As a freelancer you hold logins for your own business and often for your clients' too: hosting, social accounts, analytics, ad platforms. One reused password can expose all of them. A password manager fixes that and saves you time.

Why you need one

  • Every account gets a long, unique, random password you never have to remember.
  • You can share a login with a client or collaborator without sending it over email or chat.
  • Many managers warn you when a site you use has had a data breach.

1Password: the most polished

1Password is easy enough for non-technical clients and powerful enough for developers. Shared vaults make it simple to keep each client's logins separate, and it supports passkeys and two-factor codes.

Bitwarden: best free and open source

Bitwarden is open source and independently audited, and the free plan covers unlimited passwords on unlimited devices. Paid plans are inexpensive and add file storage, two-factor codes and sharing for teams.

NordPass: simplest to set up

NordPass focuses on a clean interface and fast setup, with breach monitoring and business plans for small teams. A good choice if you want something that just works.
