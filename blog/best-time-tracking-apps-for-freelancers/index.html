The Best Time Tracking Apps for Freelancers

5 November 2026 · 1 min read

This article contains affiliate links. If you sign up through them we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. It never affects what we recommend.

Tracking your time does two things. It makes sure you bill every hour you work, and it shows you which clients and projects actually pay well once you count all the emails and calls. Here are the best options for freelancers.

Toggl Track: the simplest timer

Toggl Track is the timer most freelancers try first. One click starts tracking, browser extensions add a timer button inside tools like Trello, Asana and GitHub, and the reports are clean enough to send to a client. There's a free plan for individuals and small teams, with paid plans for billable rates and more detailed reports.

Harvest: time tracking plus invoices

Harvest connects time tracking directly to invoicing. Log hours against a project, then turn them into an invoice and accept online payments. It's a good fit if you want one tool from timer to payment and don't need full accounting.

Clockify: the most generous free plan

Clockify lets you track unlimited time for unlimited users free. Paid plans add invoicing, scheduling and extra reports. If you're on a tight budget, start here.

Free alternatives

If you just need hours for a timesheet, the free hours calculator adds up a week of start and end times with breaks and works out your pay. For focused work sessions try the Pomodoro timer.

Tips that make tracking stick

  • Start the timer before you open your email, not after.
  • Track non-billable work too (sales, admin). It shows your real hourly rate.
  • Review once a week. If a client's real rate is far below your target, raise your price or let them go. Our hourly rate guide shows how to set the target.
