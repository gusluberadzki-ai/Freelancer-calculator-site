Do Freelancers Need a VPN? Best VPNs for Remote Work

5 November 2026 · 1 min read

VPN adverts promise a lot. For a freelancer, a VPN is useful in a few specific situations and pointless in others. Here's the honest version, and which providers are worth considering.

When a VPN helps

  • Public Wi-Fi: cafés, airports, co-working spaces and hotels. A VPN encrypts your traffic between your device and the VPN server, so others on the network can't snoop on it.
  • Travelling: some banking and client systems block logins from unfamiliar countries. Connecting through your home country can help.
  • Client requirements: some clients ask contractors to use a VPN when handling their data.

When it doesn't

A VPN won't protect you from phishing, weak passwords or malware. Most sites already use HTTPS, which encrypts the content of your traffic. If you work from home on your own network, the security benefit is small. Fix the basics first: a password manager, two-factor authentication and software updates.

NordVPN: fast and feature-rich

NordVPN has a large server network, consistently fast speeds and extras like threat protection that blocks malicious sites. It's a strong default choice.

Surfshark: best value for many devices

Surfshark allows unlimited devices on one subscription, which suits households or anyone with a laptop, phone and tablet.

Proton VPN: privacy first, with a free plan

Proton VPN is run by the Swiss company behind Proton Mail, has a strict no-logs policy that has been independently audited, and offers a free plan with no data cap, though with fewer servers and lower speeds.
