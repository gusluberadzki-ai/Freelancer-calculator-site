Best Website Builders for a Freelance Portfolio

5 November 2026 · 1 min read

A portfolio site is still the best way for clients to find you outside the marketplaces, and the best way to charge more than marketplace rates. You don't need a developer. Here's how to choose.

What a freelance portfolio needs

  • A clear line saying what you do and for whom.
  • Three to six of your best projects, each with the problem, what you did and the result.
  • Proof: client logos, testimonials, numbers.
  • One obvious way to contact you or book a call.
  • Your own domain name. It looks more professional and you keep it if you switch builders.

Framer: best for designers

Framer gives you design-tool freedom (it feels like Figma) with fast, modern sites, animations and a CMS for case studies. If visual polish matters in your work, it's the strongest choice.

Squarespace: best templates, least fuss

Squarespace has some of the most polished templates and handles hosting, security and domains for you. It's ideal if you want a beautiful site in an afternoon and don't want to tinker.

Wix: most flexible drag-and-drop

Wix lets you put anything anywhere and has apps for bookings, payments and blogs. The freedom makes it easier to build a messy site, so start from a template and stay disciplined.

Hostinger: cheapest with WordPress

Hostinger offers low-cost hosting with one-click WordPress and its own simple website builder. It's the budget option if you're happy to do a bit more setup yourself.

The free route

A static portfolio hosted on GitHub Pages or Netlify costs nothing except a domain name. A ready-made template takes care of the design so you only fill in your work.

Finishing touches

  • Compress images with the free image compressor so pages load fast.
  • Cut out product shots or headshots with the background remover.
  • Add a QR code to your business card or CV with the QR code generator.
