Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Pays Off Debt Faster?

5 November 2026 · 2 min read

If you have several debts, the order you pay them off matters. The two best-known methods are the snowball and the avalanche. Both work; they just optimise for different things.

The rules both share

  1. Pay the minimum on every debt, every month, so nothing goes into arrears.
  2. Put every spare amount towards one target debt.
  3. When it's cleared, roll its whole payment into the next target.

Debt avalanche: highest interest first

Target the debt with the highest interest rate first. Mathematically this always costs the least interest and is usually the fastest. It's the right choice if you're disciplined and the rates differ a lot, for example a credit card at 24% next to a car loan at 7%.

Debt snowball: smallest balance first

Target the smallest balance first, whatever the rate. You clear a debt quickly, which feels good and frees up a payment to roll forward. Research on behaviour suggests these quick wins help many people stick with the plan.

A worked example

DebtBalanceRateMinimum
Store card80029%25
Credit card4,50022%110
Personal loan7,0009%210

With 500 a month in total, both methods start with the store card here, because it's both the smallest and the most expensive. They then agree on the credit card too. The methods only diverge when a small balance has a low rate. Model your own debts in the credit card payoff calculator and the loan calculator.

Ways to speed up either method

  • Move high-interest card balances to a 0% balance transfer card if you can clear it before the offer ends.
  • Put windfalls (tax refunds, bonuses, a big invoice) straight onto the target debt.
  • Track every pound or dollar with a budget. Apps like YNAB are designed around exactly this.

Which should you choose?

If you trust yourself to stay the course, use the avalanche and save the most. If you've tried and given up before, use the snowball. The best method is the one you'll finish.
