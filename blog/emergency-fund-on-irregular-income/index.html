How to Build an Emergency Fund on an Irregular Income

5 November 2026 · 2 min read

When your income changes every month, an emergency fund isn't just for emergencies. It's what lets you pay rent in a quiet month, turn down a bad client and take a holiday without panic. Here's how to build one when there's no regular payday.

1. Work out your "bare bones" month

Add up only what you must pay to keep life running: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments. Leave out subscriptions, eating out and anything optional. That figure is your baseline.

2. Choose your target

Employees are usually told to save three to six months of expenses. With irregular income, aim for six to nine months, or even twelve if your work is seasonal. The free emergency fund calculator shows your target and how long it will take to get there.

3. Pay yourself a salary

This is the system that changes everything. All client payments go into one "income" account. On the same day each month, you transfer a fixed amount to your personal account, like a salary. Good months top up the income account; quiet months draw it down.

  • Set the salary at your average monthly income over the last six to twelve months, minus a little.
  • Before you pay yourself, move tax across to a separate account. See how much to set aside for taxes.

4. Save a percentage, not a fixed amount

Fixed savings targets fail in lean months. Instead, save a percentage of every payment, for example 10%, and raise it in good months. The money builds without you needing to make a decision each time.

5. Keep it where you can reach it, but not too easily

An emergency fund belongs in an instant-access savings account that pays interest, not in investments that might be down when you need them. Keeping it at a different bank from your everyday account adds just enough friction to stop impulse spending.

6. Budget with the money you have, not the money you expect

Budgeting apps built on "give every dollar a job", like YNAB, suit irregular incomes well because you only budget money that has actually arrived. Monarch Money is a good alternative if you prefer automatic categories and a household view.

Next steps

Once your fund is full, keep going. The FIRE calculator shows how long it would take for your savings to cover your spending for good.
