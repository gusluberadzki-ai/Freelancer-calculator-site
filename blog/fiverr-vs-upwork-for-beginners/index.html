Fiverr vs Upwork: Which Is Better for New Freelancers?

5 November 2026 · 1 min read

Marketplaces are the fastest way to get a first paying client without a network. Fiverr and Upwork are the two biggest, and they work in opposite ways. Here's how to pick.

The core difference

On Fiverr, you list fixed-price packages ("gigs") and buyers come to you. On Upwork, clients post jobs and you send proposals. Fiverr is a shop window; Upwork is a job board.

Fiverr suits

  • Work you can package with a clear scope: logo design, voice-overs, editing, short videos, landing pages.
  • People who'd rather not write proposals.
  • Selling the same thing many times.

Upwork suits

  • Larger, ongoing or hourly projects: development, writing retainers, marketing, virtual assistance.
  • People happy to pitch, interview and negotiate.
  • Building long-term client relationships.

Fees

Both platforms take a service fee from your earnings, and Upwork charges for "Connects" used to send proposals. Fee structures change, so check each platform's current pricing. Whatever the percentage, include it when you set prices: if a platform keeps 10–20%, your listed price needs to be that much higher to earn your target rate. Our hourly rate calculator helps you find that target.

Winning your first client

  1. Niche down. "Shopify product descriptions for skincare brands" beats "copywriter".
  2. Show finished samples. If you have no clients yet, make three example projects.
  3. Price slightly low for the first few reviews, then raise prices every few orders.
  4. Reply fast. Response time affects ranking on both platforms.
  5. Over-deliver on communication, not on unpaid extra work.

Graduate off the platform

Use marketplaces to get going, but build your own channel alongside: a portfolio site, referrals and direct clients. Direct work avoids platform fees and lets you get paid cheaply with Wise instead of PayPal.
