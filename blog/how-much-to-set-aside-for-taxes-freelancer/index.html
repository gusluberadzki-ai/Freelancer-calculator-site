How Much Should Freelancers Set Aside for Taxes?

5 November 2026 · 2 min read

The single most common money mistake new freelancers make is spending the tax. As an employee, tax was taken before you ever saw your pay. Now the whole invoice lands in your account, and a large part of it isn't yours. Here's how to decide how much to put aside.

This is general information, not tax advice. Rules differ by country and change every year, so check with your tax authority or an accountant.

The quick rule: 25–30%

If you don't know where to start, setting aside 25–30% of every payment you receive is a widely used rule of thumb for freelancers in the US, UK and much of Europe. It usually covers income tax plus self-employment tax or social contributions on moderate incomes. At higher incomes you may need more.

A better number in four steps

  1. Estimate your yearly profit: expected income minus business expenses.
  2. Look up your income tax bands for that profit, including any personal allowance.
  3. Add self-employment or social security contributions. In the US this is self-employment tax; in the UK it's Class 4 National Insurance; most countries have an equivalent.
  4. Divide total tax by total income to get your percentage, then round up a few points for safety.

The free tax set-aside calculator does the arithmetic for you.

Don't forget sales tax or VAT

If you're registered for VAT or charge sales tax, that money was never income. Move the full tax amount out the moment the client pays. Use the VAT calculator to split a payment into net and tax.

Make it automatic

The most reliable system is a separate account just for tax:

  • Open a second account, ideally a savings account that pays interest. Many business accounts such as Revolut Business let you create separate "pockets".
  • Every time a client pays, move your percentage across the same day.
  • Pay your tax bills, including any payments on account or estimated quarterly payments, from that account only.

Track it all year

Keep a simple record of income, expenses and tax set aside. Accounting apps like FreshBooks, Wave or QuickBooks categorise expenses for you, which cuts your tax bill because every allowable expense reduces your profit.

Build a buffer as well

Tax savings aren't an emergency fund. Freelance income is lumpy, so aim for several months of expenses saved separately. See our guide to building an emergency fund on irregular income.
