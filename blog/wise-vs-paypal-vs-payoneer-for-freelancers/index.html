Wise vs PayPal vs Payoneer: The Cheapest Way for Freelancers to Get Paid Abroad

5 November 2026 · 2 min read

If you work with clients in other countries, the way you receive money can quietly cost you 3–5% of every invoice. On 40,000 a year of foreign income, that's well over a thousand lost to fees and poor exchange rates. Here's how the three most popular options compare.

The hidden cost: exchange rates

Most of the cost of an international payment isn't the fee you see. It's the exchange rate. Banks and many payment apps convert at a rate worse than the real mid-market rate and keep the difference. Always compare the amount that actually lands in your account, not the advertised fee.

Wise: lowest cost for most freelancers

Wise gives you local account details in several currencies (for example a US routing and account number, a UK sort code, a euro IBAN). Clients pay you as if you were a local business, usually for free on their side. When you convert, Wise uses the mid-market rate and charges a small, clearly shown fee.

  • Good for: invoices paid by bank transfer, holding several currencies, paying for things abroad with the card.
  • Watch out for: it's not a credit facility, and some clients insist on paying through a platform.

PayPal: convenient but expensive

PayPal is everywhere, and some clients will only pay that way. But receiving international payments for goods and services carries a percentage fee plus a fixed fee, and currency conversion is marked up well above the mid-market rate. If a client insists on PayPal, consider adding the fee to the invoice where that's allowed, and withdraw in the original currency to an account that converts cheaply.

Payoneer: for marketplace sellers

Payoneer is built into many freelance marketplaces and is popular in countries where other options are limited. It offers receiving accounts in major currencies. Fees are typically higher than Wise's for conversions, so it's best when a platform pays out to it directly.

Our recommendation

Open a free Wise account and put its local account details on your invoices as the default payment method. Keep PayPal as a fallback for clients who won't pay any other way. If your business grows, a business current account like Revolut Business adds features such as multiple cards and expense management.
