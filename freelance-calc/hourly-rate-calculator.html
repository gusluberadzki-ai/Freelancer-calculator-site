How this hourly rate is calculated

Most new freelancers take their old salary, divide by 2,080 hours and end up badly underpaid. That number ignores three things: you pay your own taxes and expenses, you don't get paid holidays, and a big chunk of your week goes on unbillable work like admin, invoicing and finding clients.

This calculator starts from the take-home income you want, grosses it up for tax, adds your business expenses, then divides by the hours you can actually bill. The result is your floor: charge less and you miss your goal.

What share of time is billable?

Established freelancers with steady clients often bill 70 to 80% of their working hours. If you're just starting out and spend a lot of time on sales, 50 to 60% is more realistic.

Frequently asked questions

Should I charge hourly or per project?

Hourly is safest when the scope is unclear. Once you can estimate well, per-project pricing lets you earn more by working faster. Use your hourly floor to sanity-check every project quote.

Is this rate before or after tax?

The result is what you invoice, before tax. The calculator already adds your tax rate on top of the take-home income you entered.

How often should I raise my rate?

Review it at least once a year, and raise it whenever you're consistently fully booked.
